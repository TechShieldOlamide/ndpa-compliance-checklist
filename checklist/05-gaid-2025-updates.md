# 05. GAID 2025 Updates

The NDPA General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 (GAID) was published by the NDPC on 20 March 2025 and took effect on 19 September 2025, after a six-month transition period. It replaces the old NDPR and fills in a lot of the practical detail the Act left open. If your compliance work was built around the NDPR, this section is where to start.

## Major importance classification and registration

- [ ] You've reassessed whether your business is a data controller or processor of major importance (DCPMI) under the GAID, rather than relying on an assessment done under the NDPR.
- [ ] If you qualify, you know which tier applies to you: ultra-high, extra-high or ordinary-high level.
- [ ] If you qualify, you're registered with the NDPC and your registration details are current.

**Common gap:** businesses that checked their status once, years ago, and never looked again after the GAID changed the classification rules.

## Compliance Audit Returns (CAR)

- [ ] If you're a DCPMI, your annual compliance audit return is filed by **31 March** each year.
- [ ] If you're ultra-high or extra-high level, your audit is carried out through a licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO). Ordinary-high level DCPMIs are not required to use one.
- [ ] A new business that qualifies files its first audit within 15 months of starting operations.
- [ ] You've budgeted for the CAR filing fee, which applies from the 2026 audit cycle and scales with the volume of data you process.

**Common gap:** treating the audit return as paperwork done in March, instead of evidence gathered through the year.

## Lawful basis, consent and transparency

- [ ] Where you rely on legitimate interest, you've completed a Legitimate Interest Assessment. The GAID includes a template.
- [ ] Your privacy notice is not treated as consent. Where consent is required, you ask for it specifically.
- [ ] People who can't access your website privacy policy (at events, interviews, branches or over the phone) can still get the information in a form they understand.
- [ ] Privacy information is written with vulnerable people in mind, including children and people with limited literacy.

**Common gap:** a sign-up form that says "by continuing you accept our privacy policy" and treats that as consent for everything.

## Data Protection Impact Assessments

- [ ] You've checked the GAID's list of when a DPIA is mandatory against your current processing.
- [ ] Your DPIA format is aligned with the template in the GAID schedule.

## Cross-border transfers

- [ ] Every transfer of personal data outside Nigeria, including to cloud providers and offshore support teams, has a documented basis.
- [ ] If you use binding corporate rules or standard contractual clauses, they've been approved by the NDPC.
- [ ] You're not relying on consent alone for routine transfers. Under the GAID, fallback bases are narrow.

**Common gap:** not realising that using a foreign cloud service or SaaS tool counts as a cross-border transfer.

## DPO role and resourcing

- [ ] Your DPO has the resources, access and independence the GAID expects, not only a title.
- [ ] You can show evidence of the DPO's qualifications against the GAID's credential criteria.
