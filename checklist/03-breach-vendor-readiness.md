# 03. Breach & Vendor Readiness

The 72-hour NDPC notification clock starts the moment a qualifying breach is discovered, whether or not you're prepared. This section covers whether you'd be ready when it matters, not just whether you've thought about it in the abstract.

## Breach Response Plan

- [ ] A written breach response plan exists, not just a general understanding that "we'd deal with it."
- [ ] The plan names who is responsible for what: who assesses severity, who notifies the NDPC, who communicates to affected data subjects, who handles PR/comms.
- [ ] The plan accounts for the 72-hour NDPC notification requirement for qualifying breaches, including how you'd move that fast internally.
- [ ] The plan has been reviewed by the people responsible, not just written and filed away.
- [ ] Contact information for the NDPC and any relevant legal counsel is documented and accessible, not something you'd have to search for during an actual incident.

**Common gap:** a plan exists in a folder somewhere, but the people who'd need to execute it have never seen it or don't know it exists.

## Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)

- [ ] For high-risk processing (large-scale data collection, sensitive data categories, profiling, automated decision-making), a DPIA has been conducted.
- [ ] DPIAs are treated as a standard step before launching new high-risk processing activities, not an afterthought.
- [ ] Findings from DPIAs actually influence how the processing is designed, not just documented and ignored.

**Common gap:** businesses in fintech, lending, or health-adjacent sectors doing genuinely high-risk processing without ever having assessed the risk formally.

## Third-Party / Vendor Data Processing Agreements

- [ ] Every vendor, cloud provider, or third party that processes personal data on your behalf has a signed data processing agreement (DPA), not a verbal understanding.
- [ ] You know exactly which vendors touch personal data. (This requires the Records of Processing Activities from section 01 to actually be complete.)
- [ ] DPAs specify what the vendor can and can't do with the data, security expectations, and what happens if the vendor is breached.
- [ ] You periodically review vendor compliance, not just sign the agreement once and never revisit it.

**Common gap:** using a payment processor, cloud host, or CRM without a DPA in place, because "they're a big reputable company" was assumed to be enough.

---

**Sources:** Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023; [NDPC official guidance](https://ndpc.gov.ng). Confirm current breach notification timelines and thresholds directly with the NDPC.
