# 02. Data Subject Rights

NDPA gives people enforceable rights over their own data. This section is about whether you could actually honor those rights if someone exercised them today, not whether you have a policy that mentions them.

## Privacy Policy

- [ ] A written privacy policy exists and is accessible to anyone whose data you collect (not buried, not requiring a login to find).
- [ ] The policy reflects what you *actually* do with data, not a generic template copied from another company or jurisdiction.
- [ ] It's written in plain language a non-lawyer can understand, not just legal boilerplate.
- [ ] It's reviewed and updated when your data practices change, not left static for years.

**Common gap:** a privacy policy that describes practices from years ago, or was never customized past the template it was built from.

## Consent

- [ ] Consent is obtained clearly and affirmatively before data collection, not assumed or bundled into unrelated terms.
- [ ] You can produce a record showing consent was given (timestamp, what was agreed to, by whom), not just an assumption that it happened.
- [ ] Consent is specific to the purpose, "collecting data for marketing" is not the same as "collecting data to process an order," and bundling them without clarity is a gap.
- [ ] Withdrawal of consent is possible and the process for it is documented.

**Common gap:** "we assume they agreed because they used the service" is not demonstrable consent under NDPA.

## Data Subject Access Requests (DSARs)

- [ ] A documented internal process exists for responding to someone who asks what data you hold on them.
- [ ] A documented process exists for someone requesting deletion of their data.
- [ ] Someone in the organization knows this process exists and could execute it if a request came in today.
- [ ] You have a realistic sense of your response timeframe and whether it meets NDPA expectations.

**Common gap:** no one has ever received a DSAR, so no one has tested whether the "process" (if it exists) actually works.

## Data Minimization

- [ ] You collect only the data actually needed for the stated purpose, not data "just in case it's useful later."
- [ ] Old data that's no longer needed is deleted or archived according to a defined retention policy, not kept indefinitely by default.

---

**Sources:** Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023; [NDPC official guidance](https://ndpc.gov.ng).
